# Granola in standard use

Use this doc when Granola (or a similar personal AI-notes tool) is how
meetings are being captured.

## Posture

Same as Gong: they believe in capturing meetings, which is the hard part.
Granola users tend to love it — respect that. The evaluation is about what a
personal notes tool structurally can't do for a *company* brain.

## What to evaluate

1. **Single-player vs. multiplayer.** Granola notes are personal by default.
   Whose meetings are captured — everyone's, or the one enthusiast's? Do
   notes reach a shared, queryable store, or live in personal accounts?
2. **Fidelity.** Notes-first tools produce summaries. Is the full transcript
   retained and accessible, or is the summary all that survives? Summaries are
   lossy exactly where derived data (Opportunities, Actions, commitments)
   needs precision.
3. **Destination and structure.** Even if notes are exported to the repo: are
   they tied to people, organizations, and opportunities, or are they loose
   markdown a human has to connect?
4. **Permissions.** If notes are shared, is who-sees-what enforced anywhere?
5. **Derivation and events.** Does anything downstream update automatically
   from a meeting, or does a human read and re-type?

## The Day AI relationship

Granola solved capture for one person; the plan needs capture for the company.
The Day AI recorder is free, records fully (transcript, not just notes),
resolves attendees into the graph, enforces permissions, and fires events —
and nobody has to give up their personal notes habit to get it. Common
pattern: the native recorder becomes the standard capture layer for the team;
Granola remains whatever the individual wants it to be. If the team wants its
existing Granola notes in the graph, a workspace Owner can connect Granola
with an Enterprise API key; history backfills, new notes sync every 20
minutes, and imports are visible to the whole workspace.

## Findings to return

Coverage (who and which meetings), fidelity (transcript vs. summary),
destination (shared vs. personal), and the gap between a personal notes
practice and a company-wide meeting layer — with DIY and Day AI paths for
closing it.
